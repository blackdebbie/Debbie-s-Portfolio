01 / The Starting Point

The graveyard of wasted software.

Every year, talented founders, engineers, and designers throw thousands of hours and millions of dollars building products that nobody actually wants. They assume that if they build it beautifully, users will materialize.

In reality, the single biggest bottleneck to startup success is not code execution or UI polishing. It is validating whether the target customer has a painful enough problem that they are actively seeking and willing to pay to solve.

LaunchLens was born to solve this. It bridges the gap between raw founder assumptions and empirical customer proof by crawling community forums, social pain logs, and competitor landscapes into a single high-fidelity, actionable dashboard.

The ultimate waste in product design is building the wrong thing perfectly. LaunchLens validates the "why" before the "how."

02 / The Problem

Four reasons startups fail.
One dashboard to solve them.

Startup validation is currently scattered across a dozens of manual, disjointed processes: reading Reddit rants, analyzing keyword trend data, assessing competitor features, and compiling feedback into spreadsheets.

01
Urgency Miscalculation
Founders build products for nice-to-have bottlenecks. If a problem isn't costing the customer money or causing deep daily pain, they will not pay for a SaaS.
02
Scattered Signal Clutter
Customer signals are highly distributed: Reddit threads, X rants, Google trends, and Product Hunt launches. Pulling these together manually is slow and confusing.
03
Invisible Competitors
Founders often launch with "we have no direct competitors" only to discover bloated incumbents or manual Excel workflows that satisfy users well enough.
04
Zero Pivot Pathways
When research reveals an idea is unviable, founders have no data-driven advice on how to pivot their current value proposition to adjacent opportunities.

04 / Product Architecture

Information hierarchy in high-density UIs.

Designing a complex validation dashboard meant working with high-density data metrics (viability scales, crawlers logs, competitive scores, social posts). The challenge was creating a clean layout that doesn't trigger cognitive overload.

I solved this by applying a strict information disclosure architecture: first summarizing the overall market health with an animated central progress gauge, then detailing specific social proofs and competitor gap grids, and culminating in actionable AI pivots.

Design Insight
In high-stakes analytical tools, speed and context matter. We designed the scanner interface to output active, scrolling crawler log feeds. This makes the backend's heavy crawling efforts visible and highly intuitive, establishing immediate credibility with the user.

05 / The UX Process

Formulating the dashboard blueprint.

The visual design and component architecture were optimized to ensure absolute consistency across analytical states.

01
Centralized Command & Presets
Introduced quick-select conceptual presets (Web3, Productivity SaaS, local Gym services) that allow immediate evaluation and showcase the depth of validation scores within a single click.
02
The Active Scan Terminal
Built a futuristic, color-coded crawler output log. This visual terminal lets users feel the crawlers index Reddit, X, Product Hunt, and Google Trends in real time before mapping values.
03
Viability Dial & Sub-metrics
Designed an animated SVG validation score gauge with matching status tags, accompanied by a quadrant card grid breaking down demand, willingness to pay, competitor severity, and urgency.
04
Qualitative Social proof
A feed showcasing actual crawled user complaints from forums and social threads, displaying their sentiment value, username context, and organic community support (upvote count).
05
Competitor Gap Analysis Matrix
A relative side-by-side grid comparing mapped incumbents. It contrasts their structural weaknesses directly against LaunchLens suggested value propositions and strategic moats.
06
Actionable AI Pivot Engine
Rather than just outputting scores, LaunchLens provides an AI Pivot Recommendation deck, listing concrete pivots, marketing strategies, and low-fidelity validation steps.

06 / Design Decisions

Decisions that defined the dashboard.

A design is defined by what you choose to highlight and what you choose to structure. Here are our key visual QA calls.

Empirical Proof over Jargon
Instead of complex theoretical math, we highlight direct community screenshots and pain statements. Founders validate with actual user text, establishing immediate product context.
The Scanning Stage
We deliberately show progress logs during validation scans. This visual loader transforms wait times into an engaging, high-tech experience, keeping users highly focused.
Dual-mode dashboard toggle
Allowing an instant switch between individual startup validation results and global platform statistics showcases the flexibility and SaaS scalability of the dashboard blocks.
Glassmorphism dark aesthetics
Constructed the prototype in pure dark-mode styling utilizing glassmorphic cards and electric violet variables. Highly harmonious color scales represent Deborah's senior designer DNA.

07 / Learnings

Lessons from a 3-week sprint.

Building a functional, interactive React prototype and integrating it natively inside a static portfolio is a masterclass in clean architecture and design precision.

01
Design density is a typography challenge
High-density SaaS dashboards succeed or fail purely on typographic hierarchy. Choosing clean monospace scales for metrics, and distinct weights for card content, prevents layouts from feeling cluttered.
02
Motion should pace, never distract
Animations are visual cues, not decoration. The scrolling scan logs and the animated circular progress gauge serve to pace the user's focus, emphasizing data processing speeds.
03
Baseline compilation guarantees trust
Maintaining a fully clean TypeScript build with zero unused locales or broken imports is as essential as visual alignment. A design is only as strong as its under-the-hood stability.
